
# Spectral Statistical Arbitrage

## Research motivation

This project explores the relationship between
random matrix theory, covariance estimation and
statistical arbitrage.

Financial covariance matrices estimated from finite
samples can contain substantial statistical noise.
Random matrix theory provides a framework for
studying their spectral properties and motivating
estimation and filtering procedures.

The central research question is:

Can spectral covariance estimation improve the
stability of statistical models used in systematic
trading?

## Research objectives

1. Study empirical covariance spectra.
2. Compare classical covariance estimators with
   random-matrix-based alternatives.
3. Examine the influence of estimation noise.
4. Investigate possible applications to
   statistical arbitrage.
5. Evaluate robustness using reproducible
   experiments and out-of-sample testing.

## Research status

This is an independent research project.

Numerical results should be interpreted within
their stated assumptions and experimental setup.

A statistical relationship in historical data
does not, by itself, establish a profitable
or executable trading strategy.

## Limitations

Backtesting results may be sensitive to sample
selection, transaction costs, parameter choices,
market conditions and data leakage.

Any performance claims must be supported by
reproducible experiments.
